# M3 spikes: email, UCAP as the SMS app, previews

The measurements of `docs/plan-m3.md`, step K0, on 2026-10-15:
- **B, email:** with the owner's two test mailboxes on Hetzner;
- **C, Android's SMS role:** on the emulator;
- **D, reading other apps' conversation notifications:** on the emulator.

**A (WhatsApp)** waits for the owner, who links their own account by hand (decisions.md, M3).

The emulator: arm64, Android 16 (API 36), Google Play image, shared with the owner. For C and D, the spike apps were
their own packages, so UCAP's installed app was never touched. Afterwards everything was put back as it was:
- the SMS role with Google Messages;
- the allowed notification listeners exactly as before;
- the SMS store empty again;
- the notifications the spike caused dismissed one by one;
- both apps uninstalled;
- UCAP in front with LIVE running.

## B: email — imapflow 2.3.0, nodemailer 10.0.16, mailparser 3.9.37

Run with `node connectors/spikes/email-check.ts probe|idle|threads|sent|flags|drop|parse|bounce|clean` (or `all`;
`long <minutes>` for an IDLE that lasts). It reads `.secrets` and prints measurements only: no address, password,
server name or message id. The versions are the newest that were at least a week old: imapflow released eight times
between 4 and 15 October.

### What the test server offers

- **Connecting:** IMAP on port 993 over TLS 1.3, logged in after 0.3 s. SMTP on port 587 with STARTTLS: connect, TLS
  and AUTH verified after 0.4 s.
- **Extensions:** IDLE, CONDSTORE, QRESYNC, UIDPLUS, SPECIAL-USE, MOVE, NOTIFY, ESEARCH, SORT, THREAD=REFERENCES and
  BINARY. Not UTF8=ACCEPT, COMPRESS or IMAP4rev2.
- **Folders:** under `INBOX.`, by special use: Sent (`INBOX.Sent`), Drafts, Junk (`INBOX.spambucket`), Trash.
  imapflow switches CONDSTORE on by itself.

### Measured

| What | Measured |
|---|---|
| A send | the server's 250 after median 0.49 s (max 0.95 s, 5 sends) |
| New mail over IDLE | told to the receiving connection median 0.88 s after `sendMail` returned (max 1.3 s); 5 of 5 |
| The receiving server's checks | `Authentication-Results`: iprev, SPF and DKIM pass; DMARC skipped (the domain publishes none) |
| Headers | the subject's umlauts decoded; nodemailer's Message-ID carries the sender's domain |
| Threading | In-Reply-To and References kept through two replies, in order; the server could also thread them itself (THREAD=REFERENCES) |
| The Sent folder | **the server keeps no copy of what is sent by SMTP:** UCAP must APPEND it. APPEND of the same bytes took 36 ms; UIDPLUS gave its uid, and its Message-ID found it there |
| Read states both ways | marked read (and unread) on one connection: the other one, in IDLE, was told after 0.57 s (0.54 s), **with MODSEQ but without the UID**. Unsolicited FETCH carries the UID only once QRESYNC is enabled, so K3 enables QRESYNC. FETCH CHANGEDSINCE the old HIGHESTMODSEQ gave exactly the changed message |
| A dropped connection | the socket destroyed in IDLE: `close` at once. The old client refuses to connect again (`InstanceReused`), so K3 makes a new one. Logged in, INBOX open after 0.41 s, UIDVALIDITY unchanged, and the mail that came meanwhile fetched by UID after the last known one |
| Bounces | both undeliverable sends got a 250: the server refuses nothing at RCPT time, **not even an unknown mailbox at its own domain** |
| The bounce for a domain that cannot exist (`.invalid`) | a report after 15 s: `multipart/report` (delivery-status), the original attached as `message/rfc822`, and its Message-ID matched the send. The spike did not get Action and Status out of its delivery-status part through mailparser's attachments: K3 reads that part itself |
| An unknown mailbox at the testers' own domain | **no report within 15 minutes**, and the mail is in neither test mailbox. The domain may collect unknown addresses in another mailbox, which cannot be told from outside. So a wrong address is not always noticed, and UCAP never says a mail "kam an" |
| Searching on the server | its full-text search matches whole words: SEARCH BODY for `ucap-spike-` found nothing, the whole token found the report. K3 searches its own copy in Postgres, never the server's |

### Parsing (mailparser, invented samples, APPENDed and fetched back)

- **Reply headers:** a first cut of K3's split found the new text and the quoted part in every common form: Gmail (de,
  en), Apple Mail ("> Am … schrieb …:"), Thunderbird ("Am 12.10.26 um 10:00 schrieb …:"), Outlook (de "Von: …
  Gesendet: …", en "-----Original Message-----") and a forward ("-------- Weitergeleitete Nachricht --------").
- **Signatures:** "-- " splits off the signature.
- **Outlook's HTML-only reply:** as text it leaves a line of dashes (its `<hr>`) at the end of the new part, which must
  be trimmed.
- **mailparser's HTML-to-text is not for speech.** On a newsletter it:
  - **keeps the hidden preheader** ("Nur heute: 20 % auf alles", `display:none`);
  - names images by their URL, `[https://…/logo.png]`;
  - runs table cells together ("Tulpen3,49 €Narzissen2,99 €");
  - writes links as "Jetzt ansehen [https://…?utm_source=…]";
  - keeps the tracking pixel as `[https://…/p.gif]`.

  K3 renders HTML mail to speakable text itself: hidden elements dropped, images by their alt text or not at all, cells
  apart, links by their words, no URLs.
- **Old charsets:** ISO-8859-1 with quoted-printable, and encoded words in From and Subject, were decoded correctly
  ("Jürgen Müller", "Straße").
- **Attachments:** each with its name, type and size. An inline picture is marked as such (`cid`, related), apart from
  the PDF.
- **List headers:** mailparser gathers `List-*` under one header, `list` (`{ unsubscribe: { url }, id: { name } }`,
  checked offline); `Precedence: bulk` is its own. Newsletters are recognised by these.
- Parsing took 0–39 ms per mail, the newsletter the longest.

### Learned

- **nodemailer resolves the host with its own DNS queries** (`dns.resolve4/6`, through c-ares) before the system's
  resolver. Where direct DNS is blocked (this Mac's Claude Code sandbox: 27 s per query), the first SMTP connection
  waited two minutes; given an address and `servername`, it resolves nothing.
  - In Docker the queries work, but K3 resolves through the system (`dns.lookup`) and passes the address, so a slow
    resolver never holds a send.
- **imapflow and nodemailer log commands at their debug levels** (LOGIN carries the password): both run with
  `logger: false`. imapflow brings pino for that.
- **The `flags` event has no UID** without QRESYNC (above).
- **No bounce at RCPT time:** a typo in an address is noticed only by the report that follows (when one comes), so "nicht
  zugestellt" is told later, matched by Message-ID.

## C: UCAP as the SMS app — Android's SMS role on the emulator

Run with `sh tools/android/spikes/m3/build.sh`, then `node tools/android/spikes/m3/drive.ts install`, then `role`,
`sms`, `giveback`, and finally `restore`:
- The spike app (`de.schaefchens.ucap.spike`) has the role's four parts: SMS and MMS delivery, a compose screen for
  other apps, and quick replies to a call.
- It is built with the SDK's own tools (aapt2, javac, d8, apksigner), without Gradle.
- It is driven over adb, and logs ids, counts, times and lengths only.

| What | Measured |
|---|---|
| Android's dialog | "Set UCAP Spike as your default SMS app?": a list of the SMS apps with the current one (Messages, "Current default") **preselected**. "Set as default" stays **disabled** until UCAP is chosen in the list, so it takes two deliberate choices. With TalkBack: not tried |
| What the role grants | SMS and MMS (RECEIVE_SMS, READ_SMS, SEND_SMS, RECEIVE_MMS, RECEIVE_WAP_PUSH); also READ_CONTACTS, READ_PHONE_STATE, CALL_PHONE, READ_CALL_LOG, POST_NOTIFICATIONS, RECORD_AUDIO and CAMERA. Not READ_PHONE_NUMBERS (the phone's own number came anyway, through READ_PHONE_STATE) |
| What Play allows of it | for the default SMS app only the SMS permissions. READ_CALL_LOG is the phone app's and the assistant app's (Play's "Permitted uses of the SMS and Call Log permission groups"), so "Wer hat angerufen?" stays out of M3 although Android grants the call log |
| An SMS in | SMS_DELIVER reaches the SMS app only, and it alone writes the message into Android's store: no duplicate from Google Messages (6 messages in the store, as sent and received) |
| An SMS out | to the emulator's own number: the sent report after 19 ms, back as an incoming SMS after about 1 s, the delivery report (status 0) after 2.0 s |
| A long SMS | 202 characters with umlauts (in GSM's own alphabet, so 7-bit): 2 parts, a sent and a delivery report for each, arrived joined |
| The SMS centre's time | an hour off on the emulator. UCAP orders and ages SMS by when they arrived, and keeps the centre's time apart |
| Other apps' "write an SMS" | `SENDTO smsto:` opens the SMS app's compose screen directly, no chooser |
| Giving the role back | Android's Default apps → SMS app → Messages: one tap, no dialog. **Every permission the role granted is taken away, and Android ends the app's processes** (new process ids), so UCAP must come back by itself after it. The history stays in Android's store for Google Messages |
| Google Messages' own way back | opening it shows its welcome first (sign in, or use it without an account). Not gone through: it would change the shared emulator's Google Messages |
| Deleting from the store | only the SMS app may: `adb shell content delete` changed nothing. The spike took the role for a moment (`cmd role add-role-holder`) to delete what it had made |

**Not on the emulator:** MMS from a carrier (it has no MMS centre), RCS, a real network's reports, dual SIM, and the
dialog with TalkBack under real fingers. They stay open until a real phone with a SIM is available (plan-m3.md, Risks).

## D: previews — a notification listener in a process of its own

With the same tools: `drive.ts previews`. A stand-in messenger (`de.schaefchens.ucap.spike.messenger`) posts as
messengers do: MessagingStyle, the last seven messages of the conversation again with every new one, a reply action
and a mark-as-read action, and the person's reply shown as their own message.

| What | Measured |
|---|---|
| Each message once | 5 messages posted 2 s apart, each notification carrying the whole conversation again (1 to 5 messages): each time exactly one was new |
| How fast | from a message's time to the listener: 207–224 ms (median 210, 5 of 5) |
| Groups | the conversation's title and "group" are there; each message names its sender |
| A reply | through the notification's own reply action: handed over in 2 ms. Confirmed when the messenger showed it as the person's own message, 214 ms later |
| Read | the mark-as-read action: the messenger took its notification away (reason "app cancel") |
| A stopped messenger | force-stop removed its notifications and cancelled their actions: the reply failed (`CanceledException`), so it is told as not sent |
| One-time codes | **not redacted on this emulator**: a German and an English code arrived in full, as lookup saw on 2026-10-13. Android documents redaction for listeners from 15 on, so UCAP works with both and never says a code by itself |
| A crash in the listener's process | the main process (where UCAP's help lives) ran on and answered at once. Android started the listener again after about 1 s |
| After that restart | the listener reported the conversations still showing as new again: 10 messages told twice. **"Each message once" belongs on the server**, by a stable key (the app, the conversation's notification key, the sender, the message's time, its text), not in the listener's memory |
| An SMS through Google Messages' notification (before the role moved) | readable 1.9 s after it arrived, with reply and read actions. Google Messages first posts a "service" notification while it works, to be ignored |
| Android's group summary | a notification of the same app without messages, to be ignored |

## Choices

- **K3 (email):**
  - **Clients and connections:** one imapflow client per account in IDLE on INBOX, with QRESYNC on. After any
    `close`, a new client resumes from the last UID and HIGHESTMODSEQ, and a UIDVALIDITY change refetches.
  - **Sending:** nodemailer with the address resolved by the system. Then APPEND to the Sent folder (by special use),
    except where the provider copies it itself (Gmail).
  - **Bounces:** matched by the original's Message-ID.
  - **Text:** the new text, the quoted part and the signature kept apart, and K3's own HTML-to-speech step.
  - **Newsletters:** recognised by `list` and `Precedence`.
- **K4 (the SMS role):**
  - The role's four parts, as in the spike. The store is written by UCAP, and SMS are ordered by arrival.
  - After the role goes back, UCAP restarts by itself (Android ends it). The way back is Android's Default apps page,
    which UCAP opens.
  - **The dialog needs two choices**, so a helper may need to do it with the person.
- **K5 (previews):**
  - **The listener:** in a process of its own. It ignores "service" notifications and group summaries.
  - **Messages and conversations:** the server keeps each message once by a stable key, and replies go back only through
    the conversation's own notification.
  - **States:** a reply counts as sent once the messenger shows it as the person's own; it failed when its action was
    cancelled.
  - **One-time codes** are marked and never said by themselves.
